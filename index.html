Cryptography & distributed computing

Kecheng Shi

PhD student at CISPA and Saarland University

I study how authentication changes what is possible in distributed consensus. Who needs to know whose verification key, and how much initial authentication does agreement require? I also work on agreement with omission faults and on broadcast protocols that stop early when few parties fail.

  • Authentication and distributed consensus
  • Omission faults
  • Early stopping

Research

Current questions

  • What can digital signatures achieve when only some parties have registered keys?
  • When is agreement possible if a party can miss both incoming and outgoing messages?
  • How quickly can a protocol terminate when fewer faults occur than it was designed to tolerate?

Background

I am a doctoral student at Saarland University and the CISPA Helmholtz Center for Information Security, advised by Julian Loss.

Before joining CISPA in 2023, I studied mathematics at the Southern University of Science and Technology in Shenzhen, where I later worked as a research assistant.

Omission Faults

  1. 2026FC

    OmniBA: Round-Efficient BA with Quadratic Communication under Mixed Faults

    Simon Holmgaard Kamp, Julian Loss, Kartik Nayak, and Kecheng Shi

    Our protocol tolerates Byzantine and omission faults with expected quadratic communication. It takes a constant number of rounds in expectation when a constant fraction of parties are fault-free.

    ePrintConference program
  2. 2024TCC

    Consensus in the Presence of Overlapping Faults and Total Omission

    Julian Loss, Kecheng Shi, and Gilad Stern

    We give an agreement protocol for overlapping send and receive faults and study what agreement can guarantee when every party may experience omissions.

    ePrintProceedings
